Introduction

We invite you to participate in a research study on how descriptions of digital media forensic techniques are understood by readers. These are computational methods for analyzing images and videos as evidence, for example, to judge whether a video has been altered or AI-generated, to trace the model that produced it, or to check whether it is being used misleadingly. You will read a short introduction of the technique description formats, read several technique descriptions, and decide their fits to the described scenarios. The survey takes about 45 minutes and is designed to be completed online.

Participation is entirely voluntary. If any part of this form or the study is unclear, please contact the research team using the e-mail addresses or phone numbers listed at the end of this document.

Purpose

The goal of this study is to understand how effectively written descriptions of digital media forensic techniques communicate what those techniques can and cannot do. You will read descriptions presented in different formats and judge whether each technique applies to a described scenario; your responses will help us identify where such descriptions support sound judgments and where they leave readers uncertain. The aggregated findings will be used to evaluate and refine a proposed Visual Media Forensics Ontology, a structured vocabulary for describing forensic techniques, and to guide how technique capabilities are documented in research and practice.

Duration

We expect the study session to take around 45 minutes.

Procedures

Participants in this study will be asked to complete an online survey hosted on ReVISit, lasting approximately 45 minutes in a single session without interruption. After you provide consent, you will read about how technique descriptions are presented in this study. You will then complete three sections. In the first, you will read eight short scenarios, each paired with one technique, and judge whether that technique applies to the scenario. In the second, you will read four scenarios and select the most suitable technique from four options. In the third, you will answer Likert-scale and open-ended questions about your experience. There is no time limit for this survey. When you finish, you will receive payment through Prolific after the research team approves your submission.

Possible risks / discomforts

No personally identifying information will be collected through the survey. We do not anticipate any risks or discomforts associated with participation beyond those encountered in everyday life. However, if you experience any discomfort while completing the survey, you may choose to skip any question or stop participating at any time.

Benefits

There are no direct benefits to you from taking part in this study. The information you provide will help researchers understand how the capabilities of digital media forensic techniques can be described more clearly, which may benefit people who need to select appropriate techniques for their work in the future.

Compensation

You will receive $15 through Prolific for completing this survey, in addition to the $0.50 paid for the eligibility screener.

The survey has three sections. If you are unable to finish, you can message the research team through Prolific to request payment for the sections you completed ($5 per completed section).

In line with Prolific's policies, a submission may be rejected without payment only if it contains no study data or shows clear evidence that the study was not genuinely attempted.

Voluntary participation

Participation in this research study is voluntary. You have the right to decline participation in any or all study procedures or quit at any time with no consequence.

Furthermore, please note that you are not required to answer any questions that you do not feel comfortable answering during the course of this study. Brief explanations, however, make your answer especially valuable. If you’re willing, we would appreciate a sentence or two wherever the survey invites you to share the reasons behind your selections or provide feedback to us.

Confidentiality

Only Y. Kelly Wu, Saniat Sohrawardi, Dr. Ersin Uzun, and Dr. Matthew Wright will have access to all of the data collected from participants. In any publications or presentations of our research, we will use a coded name for your answers. Data associated with this study will be maintained for 3 years after the work gets published and will be stored in the ESL Global Cybersecurity Institute at RIT.

If in the unlikely event, it becomes necessary for the RIT Institutional Review Board to review your research records, then RIT will protect the confidentiality of those records to the extent permitted by law.

Contact for questions

You are encouraged to contact us with any questions or concerns that you might have, even after leaving this study. Furthermore, we will gladly provide you with a debriefing regarding this form, the program, or the research if you desire.
