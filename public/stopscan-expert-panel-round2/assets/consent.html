Study title: Structured Expert Evaluation of STOP&SCAN, a Framework for Evaluating Potentially Synthetic Media

This round: Expert review of the revised STOP&SCAN framework

You are being asked to take part in the second round of a research study. This document explains what this round involves so you can decide whether you want to participate. Please read it carefully and email the Principal Investigator with any questions before you decide. Taking part is entirely voluntary.

Why you are being asked

You are receiving this because you were invited to the first round of this study, on the basis of your professional work in digital media forensics, misinformation research, media literacy education, or fact-checking and verification journalism. You are welcome to take part whether or not you took part in the first round.

What this round is about

STOP&SCAN is a framework intended to help non-experts evaluate images, video, and audio that may be AI-generated or misleading. In the first round, professionals in these four fields read worked evaluations of real cases and critiqued the framework. We have since revised STOP&SCAN in response to what they told us.

This round asks whether those revisions are the right ones. We show you the revised framework, each change we made, why we made it, and the first-round result that led to it, and we ask how far you agree with each change. Repeating a structured question to the same group of experts with feedback from the previous round is known as a Delphi study.

As before, we want your criticism. If a change does not fix the problem it was meant to fix, or creates a new one, that is the most useful thing you can tell us.

What you will be asked to do

This round is a single online questionnaire of about 10 to 15 minutes, a little longer if you did not take part in the first round. You can take part at whatever time and place suits you. Nobody will observe you, and there is no scheduled appointment. It runs on the same study platform as the first round.

You will:

  1. Tell us whether you took part in the first round. If you did not, you will answer the same short background questions the first round used. These are your area of expertise, roughly how long you have worked in it (given as a range, not an exact figure), and how familiar you are with existing verification methods.
  2. Read the revised STOP&SCAN framework.
  3. For each change, read what changed, why we changed it, and the first-round result behind it, then rate how far you agree with the change on a five-point scale from strongly disagree to strongly agree. If you do not agree with a change, we ask you briefly what you would do instead. Otherwise comments are optional.
  4. Answer one or two short open questions about the revised framework as a whole.
  5. Choose whether you would like to be named in the acknowledgments of publications from this study. This is optional and is explained below.

If you did not take part in the first round

You are still welcome to take part. Because you will not have read the worked cases the first round used, we report your responses separately from those of first-round participants.

What we record

Your ratings and written responses, whether you took part in the first round, your background categories if you did not, your acknowledgment choice, and how long you spent on each page. We do not ask for your name or email address unless you choose to be acknowledged. We do not record your keystrokes, your screen, audio, or video, and we do not record your IP address. We do not link your answers in this round to your answers in the first round.

Being named in the acknowledgments (optional)

Some experts in the first round asked whether they could be credited for their contribution. At the end of the questionnaire you can choose to be listed by name in the acknowledgments of publications from this study, as a member of the expert panel. If you choose this, we ask how you would like your name, and if you wish your affiliation, to appear. If you leave the question blank or choose not to be named, you will not be named.

Being named means that readers will know you served on the panel. In publications, named panelists are listed alphabetically, without field, years of experience, or participant code, and we do not attribute any rating, response, or quotation to a named person.

The name you give is stored with your responses in this round, and members of the research team will be able to see it. If you change your mind about being named, email the Principal Investigator.

Is there any payment?

No. There is no payment, gift card, or other compensation. We are asking for your professional judgment as a colleague, and we recognize that is a real request on your time. If you would find it useful, we will send you a summary of what we find.

Risks

We believe the risks to you are minimal. There are three possibilities.

Being identified from your responses

The professional community you work in is small, and a description of your field and experience could be enough for a knowledgeable reader to guess who you are. To reduce this, this round does not ask your name unless you choose acknowledgment, does not ask where you work, and collects experience only as a broad range. We describe participants in publication only by one of four broad fields, and we screen any quotation and reword it where necessary to remove distinctive phrasing or references to specific work you have done. We will not report any combination of details that would narrow you down to fewer than three plausible people. We cannot promise this risk is zero. If there is anything you write that you would prefer we never quote, tell us in your response and we will not quote it.

Being named, if you choose it

If you choose acknowledgment, the fact that you served on the panel becomes public, and the research team will see your name alongside your responses in this round. Your views are not attributed to you by name in any publication.

Professional discomfort

You are being asked to judge changes to a framework in your own field. There are no right answers, we are not scoring you, and no individual responses will be reported. Critical responses are what we are hoping for.

Benefits

We cannot promise you any direct benefit. You may find it professionally interesting to see how expert criticism changed the framework, and you may request a summary of the findings. The broader purpose is to avoid putting flawed guidance in front of the public.

Your right to stop

Participation is entirely voluntary. You may skip any question. You may stop at any time, for any reason or no reason, by closing the browser. Nothing happens if you do, you do not need to tell us why, and there is nothing to forfeit.

Unless you give your name, this round does not record who you are, so once you submit the questionnaire we cannot tell which responses are yours and cannot delete them on request. If you gave your name, email the Principal Investigator and we will delete your responses.

Declining will not affect any professional relationship you have with anyone on the research team. If you would rather not take part, you do not need to reply. We are sending this invitation once and will not send a reminder.

How your information is protected

Your responses in this round are stored without your name unless you choose to be acknowledged. We hold your name and email address only because you were invited to the first round. We use them in this round only to send you this single invitation, and they are kept in the same separate, restricted file as before and deleted on the schedule described in the first-round consent. If you choose to be acknowledged, your name is kept until the last publication from this study has appeared and then deleted from the data.

Data are stored on RIT-managed, encrypted, password-and-multi-factor-protected institutional storage. We do not send your responses by email. No commercial AI system, language model, or analytics service outside RIT processes your responses at any stage. Analysis is carried out by the research team, supported where useful by AI systems hosted internally on RIT-managed infrastructure. Your responses are not sent to any outside service.

This study is run by RIT. Your responses are available to the research team named above, including the co-investigator at the University of Bologna, who works on this study as approved study personnel and takes part in the analysis and writing. We may publish de-identified findings and quotations in academic articles and conference proceedings and may deposit de-identified data in a public research repository. Written responses will only be deposited if screening confirms they cannot identify you.
