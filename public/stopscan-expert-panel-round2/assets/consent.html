We invite you to take part in the second round of a research study. This document explains the study so you can decide whether to join. Please read it carefully. Email the Principal Investigator if you have questions. Taking part is your choice.

Why you are being asked

We invited you because of your professional work. Your work is in digital media forensics, misinformation research, media literacy education, or fact-checking and verification journalism. You may join this round even if you did not complete the first round.

What this round is about

STOP&SCAN helps non-experts check images, video, and audio that may be made by AI or may be misleading. In the first round, professionals reviewed examples based on real cases. They then gave us feedback on the framework. We used their feedback to revise STOP&SCAN.

This round asks whether we made the right changes. We will show you each change, why we made it, and the Round 1 result behind it. You will rate how much you agree with each change. This process is called a Delphi study. In a Delphi study, researchers ask experts structured questions over more than one round. Experts also see feedback from the earlier round.

We want honest criticism. Please tell us if a change does not fix the problem or creates a new problem.

What you will be asked to do

This round is one online questionnaire. It takes about 10 to 15 minutes. It may take a little longer if you did not complete Round 1. You may complete it at any time and place. Nobody will watch you, and there is no appointment.

You will:

  1. Tell us whether you completed Round 1. If you did not, you will answer a few short background questions. We ask about your area of expertise, your range of work experience, and your knowledge of existing verification methods.
  2. Read the revised STOP&SCAN framework.
  3. For each change, read what changed and why. You will also see the Round 1 result behind the change. Rate each change on a five-point scale from strongly disagree to strongly agree. If you choose 1, 2, or 3, briefly tell us what you would change. Other comments are optional.
  4. Answer one or two short open questions about the revised framework as a whole.
  5. Choose whether you would like to be named in the acknowledgments of publications from this study. This is optional and is explained below.

If you did not take part in the first round

You may still take part. You did not see the case examples from Round 1, so we will report your responses separately from the responses of Round 1 participants.

What we record

We record your ratings, written answers, and time on each page. We also record whether you completed Round 1. If you did not, we record your background categories. We record your choice about being named in the acknowledgments. We do not ask for your name or email unless you choose to be named. We do not record your keystrokes, screen, audio, video, or IP address. We do not connect your answers in this round to your Round 1 answers.

Being named in the acknowledgments (optional)

Some Round 1 experts asked to receive credit for their work. At the end, you may choose to be named as a member of the expert panel in study publications. If you choose yes, we ask how your name should appear. You may also give an affiliation. If you leave the question blank or choose no, we will not name you.

If you choose to be named, readers will know that you served on the panel. We list names in alphabetical order. We do not list your field, years of experience, or participant code. We do not connect any rating, answer, or quotation to your name.

The name you give is stored with your responses in this round, and members of the research team will be able to see it. If you change your mind about being named, email the Principal Investigator.

Is there any payment?

No. There is no payment, gift card, or other reward. We know that this study takes your time. We can send you a summary of the results if you would like one.

Risks

We believe the risks to you are minimal. There are three possibilities.

Being identified from your responses

Your professional community is small. A reader who knows the field might guess who you are from your experience and area of work. We take several steps to reduce this risk. We do not ask for your name unless you choose to be named. We do not ask where you work. We collect your experience as a broad range. In publications, we use only broad fields to describe participants. We check quotations and may change identifying wording or details about your work. We will not report a group of details if it could narrow your identity to fewer than three possible people. We cannot remove all risk. If you do not want us to quote something you write, say so in your answer. We will not quote it.

Being named, if you choose it

If you choose to be named, the public will know that you served on the panel. The research team will also see your name with your answers from this round. We will not connect your views to your name in any publication.

Professional discomfort

We ask you to judge a framework related to your field. There are no right answers, and we are not scoring you. We will not report your individual answers. We welcome critical feedback.

Benefits

We cannot promise any direct benefit to you. You may find it useful to see how expert feedback changed the framework. You may also ask for a summary of the findings. The wider goal is to avoid giving the public guidance that does not work well.

Your right to stop

Taking part is your choice. You may skip any question. You may stop at any time by closing the browser. You do not need to give a reason. There is no penalty for stopping.

We cannot identify your answers unless you give us your name. If you do not give your name, we cannot find or delete your answers after you submit them. If you give your name, you may email the Principal Investigator and ask us to delete your answers.

Choosing not to join will not affect any professional relationship with the research team. If you do not want to join, you do not need to reply. We will send this invitation once and will not send a reminder.

How your information is protected

We store your answers without your name unless you choose to be named. We already have your name and email because we invited you to Round 1. For this round, we use them only to send this invitation. We keep them in a separate file with limited access. We will delete them on the schedule in the Round 1 consent form. If you choose to be named, we keep that name until the last study publication appears. We then delete it from the data.

We store the data on encrypted systems managed by RIT. These systems use passwords and multi-factor authentication. We do not send your answers by email. We do not send your answers to any commercial AI system, language model, analytics service, or other outside service. The research team analyses the data. It may use AI systems hosted inside RIT.

RIT runs this study. The research team named above can access your answers. This includes the co-investigator at the University of Bologna, who is approved to work on this RIT study. The team analyses the data and writes the publications. We may publish findings and quotations that do not identify you. We may also place data that does not identify you in a public research repository. We will include written answers only after checking that they cannot identify you.

For participants in Europe (EEA, UK, or Switzerland)

This section applies if you are in the European Economic Area (EEA), the United Kingdom, or Switzerland. The EEA includes EU countries, Iceland, Liechtenstein, and Norway. The EU General Data Protection Regulation applies in the EEA. The UK GDPR applies in the United Kingdom. The Federal Act on Data Protection applies in Switzerland. The protections below are the same in each place.

  • Controller. Rochester Institute of Technology (RIT) controls the data. This means RIT decides how the data are used and is responsible for them. The University of Bologna is not a controller. Its co-investigator may access the data only as an approved member of the RIT study team.
  • Lawful basis. Your explicit consent, given below.
  • Data collected in this round. We collect whether you completed Round 1, your ratings, your written answers, and the time you spend on each page. If you did not complete Round 1, we also collect your professional field and experience range. We collect your name and optional affiliation only if you choose to be named. This information is stored with your answers. We already have your name and email from the Round 1 invitation. We use them only to send this invitation.
  • How long we keep the data. We delete Round 1 contact details within twelve months of the study start, as stated in the Round 1 consent form. If you give a name for the acknowledgments, we keep it until the last study publication appears. We then delete it. We keep answers that do not identify you for three years after publication.
  • Transfer to the United States. We will transfer your data to the United States. The EU and UK have not made an adequacy decision that covers this transfer. We make the transfer only with your clear consent.
  • Your rights. You may ask to see, correct, or delete your data. You may ask us to limit how we use it. You may withdraw your consent at any time, without giving a reason or receiving a penalty. This does not change whether our earlier use of your data was lawful. To use any of these rights, email the Principal Investigator. We can find your answers only if you gave us your name.
  • You may make a complaint to your national data protection authority.
